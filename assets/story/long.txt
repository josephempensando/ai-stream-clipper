Every morning, the woman opened the small repair shop beside the harbor before the fishing boats returned.

She liked those quiet minutes, when the birds were still deciding where to land.

The street smelled of rain.

On the shelf above her workbench stood a little brass lantern.

It had belonged to her grandmother, who once kept the harbor light during a winter storm.

One afternoon, a child brought in a wooden boat with a broken sail.

The woman could have replaced the whole mast, but the child wanted to keep the original wood.

It was the first thing he had ever built with his father.

So she searched her drawers for a smaller piece that would support the crack without hiding it.

Together they wrapped the mast with a thin strip of copper and tested the sail in the breeze from the open door.

By evening, the rain had returned.

The child left with his boat tucked safely under his coat, and the woman began closing the shop.

Then she noticed a fishing boat waiting beyond the harbor wall.

Its engine was quiet, and its deck lights had gone dark.

The usual harbor signal was hidden behind a sheet of heavy rain.

The woman took the brass lantern from the shelf, lit it, and walked to the end of the pier.

She held it high and moved it slowly from side to side.

After a while, a small light answered from the water.

The boat turned toward the entrance, following her steady signal.

When the crew finally stepped ashore, she recognized the child's father among them.

He looked at the lantern, then at the little copper strip still resting on her sleeve.

Neither repair had been large, but both had helped someone find their way home.
